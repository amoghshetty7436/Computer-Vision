2. Problem statement

Structure from Motion (SfM) recovers camera poses (position and orientation) and a sparse 3D point cloud from an unordered set of overlapping photographs. It is the first stage of most 3D pipelines: photogrammetry, NeRF and Gaussian splatting, visual localisation for AR and robots, and digital heritage.

COLMAP (Schönberger & Frahm, Structure-from-Motion Revisited, CVPR 2016) is the standard implementation and the paper under study. Its evaluation uses clean internet photo collections. It does not measure behaviour under:

  • adverse weather and illumination, including desert conditions (sandstorm, heat shimmer, harsh sun);
  • progressively stronger image degradation, and in particular content produced by generative models (inpainting, GAN and diffusion re-synthesis), which is now common in shared photos;
  • super-resolution used as a pre-processing step;
  • dense crowds of moving people;
  • mixtures of good and bad images, alternative features and mappers, and cross-condition localisation.

Research question. Under which real-world conditions does COLMAP fail, how does failure grow with severity, which stage of the pipeline is responsible, and do common mitigations or newer learned components help? For every experiment we state in advance why it might fail and why it might work, then measure which happened.

Brief from the course staff. (1) desert scenes and weather-stratified evaluation; (2) progressively added analytical and generative noise; (3) super-resolution before SfM; (4) generated tourist crowds. We added (5) partial contamination, (6) a features × matcher × mapper ablation and (7) cross-season localisation.

3. Background: how SfM works

  1. Feature extraction. SIFT finds blob-like keypoints at many scales (Difference-of-Gaussian extrema across octaves) and describes each with a 128-D gradient histogram that is invariant to brightness, contrast, scale and rotation. We keep up to 8,192 per image.
  2. Matching. Exhaustive: every image pair is compared. Each descriptor is matched to its nearest neighbour; Lowe's ratio test rejects matches whose best candidate is barely better than the second best (ambiguous).
  3. Geometric verification (RANSAC). For each pair COLMAP estimates an essential matrix (5-point solver), a fundamental matrix (7-point) and a homography (4-point) with LO-RANSAC. A pair is verified if enough matches agree (inliers; at least 15 by default).
  4. Incremental reconstruction. Start from a well-connected pair, triangulate points, then repeatedly register the next image by solving its pose from 2D–3D correspondences (P3P + RANSAC), triangulate its new points, and run bundle adjustment.
  5. Bundle adjustment. Non-linear least squares over all camera poses, intrinsics and 3D points, minimising reprojection error.

3.1 RANSAC in detail

RANSAC repeatedly samples a minimal set of matches, fits a model, counts the matches that agree within a pixel threshold, and keeps the model with the most inliers. To be 99% sure of drawing at least one all-inlier sample of size s when a fraction w of matches is correct, it needs N = log(0.01) / log(1 − ws) samples. For the 5-point solver that is about 145 samples at w = 0.5, about 14,000 at w = 0.2 and about 460,000 at w = 0.1, so performance collapses abruptly at low inlier ratios. RANSAC assumes outliers are random. Consistent outliers, such as repeated or symmetric structure, the same pasted person in two views, or JPEG block corners, can form a winning but wrong model. This explains the two failure types we see throughout: loud (too few inliers, images fail to register) and silent (consistent wrong inliers, images register in the wrong place).

3.2 Registration

An image is registered when the mapper has estimated its pose and added it to the model. Images that never gather enough verified 2D–3D correspondences stay unregistered. The registered fraction is registered images divided by input images, measured on the largest model; images in smaller disconnected models count as unregistered.

3.3 Global SfM

GLOMAP (Pan et al., ECCV 2024; built into COLMAP 4) estimates all rotations jointly by rotation averaging over the view graph, then all positions by global positioning, then bundle adjustment. It is faster and avoids drift, but a wrong but verified pair enters the global solve directly instead of being checked against an existing model.

6. Evaluation: ground truth and metrics

Ground truth. COLMAP scenes: the authors' full-resolution reconstruction from all images (pseudo ground truth). Because it was made with SIFT it could favour SIFT slightly, but the learned-feature errors we report are 80°–135° flips, far beyond any such bias. Boreas: GNSS/INS poses in a shared ENU frame.

Pose AUC@τ (main metric). For every pair (i, j) of input images:

  1. Compute the relative pose from the estimate and from ground truth: Rrel = RjRiT, trel = tj − Rrelti (world-to-camera convention).
  2. Rotation error = angle of Rrel,estTRrel,gt. Translation error = unsigned angle between translation directions (scale is unobservable).
  3. Pair error = max(rotation error, translation error). If either image is unregistered, the error is ∞.
  4. AUC@τ = mean over 100 thresholds x in (0, τ] of the fraction of pairs with error ≤ x. This equals the mean over pairs of max(0, 1 − error/τ), so a 1° pair earns 0.9 and a 9° pair 0.1 at τ = 10°.

Relative poses avoid aligning coordinate frames or recovering scale. Counting unregistered pairs as failures follows the Image Matching Challenge (Jin et al., IJCV 2021) and stops a method from scoring well by discarding hard images. We log AUC@5, @10 and @20 and report @10.

Worked example. Five pairs with errors 0.5°, 1.5°, 4°, 12° and one unregistered image give credits 0.95, 0.85, 0.60, 0, 0, so AUC@10 = 0.48.

Secondary metrics. Registered fraction; ATE (camera-centre RMSE after a robust Sim(3) alignment, Umeyama inside RANSAC over 3-point samples, divided by the median distance of reference centres from their centroid); number of 3D points; mean track length; mean reprojection error; per-stage timings. Reprojection error measures internal consistency only, and can be low on a wrong model.

7. Experimental protocol

  • Seeds. Each synthetic condition runs with seeds 0, 1, 2 on both scenes (n = 6). The seed changes the corruption random draws (hashed per image, condition, severity and seed) and COLMAP's RANSAC seed. SD ×4 upscaling used seed 0 only (n = 2), because of GPU cost.
  • Severity. Five levels per corruption. Most tables report s1, s3, s5. Parameters are listed with each experiment.
  • Uncertainty. 95% bootstrap confidence intervals over runs, shown in brackets.
  • Clean variance. Gerrard Hall clean AUC@10 across seeds: 0.969, 0.972, 0.965. Across all 42 clean runs: mean 0.959, std 0.047, min 0.670 (one Gerrard Hall run).
  • Resumability. Every finished run is appended to a CSV and skipped on restart. Generative images are cached and generated per image with atomic writes. Duplicate rows are removed in analysis on (experiment, scene, condition, seed, sequence, anchor, perturbation, severity).

8. E1 Weather and desert

Why it might fail

Fog, haze and dust remove contrast so gradients fall below SIFT's threshold. Snowflakes and rain streaks are blob-like and create false keypoints. Night adds photon noise and blur. Heat shimmer warps each view differently, breaking epipolar geometry. Clipped highlights have no gradients.

Why it might work

SIFT normalises brightness and contrast and works across scales; daytime weather barely hurt urban localisation in Sattler et al. (2018). RANSAC rejects random false matches, and only a few hundred good matches per pair are needed.

Findings

  • Night is the only real condition that breaks SfM (0.02, CI 0.01–0.04). Half the night frames register, but with wrong poses.
  • Daytime rain and snow do not hurt (0.87, 0.88), in line with Sattler et al. (2018).
  • Place matters more than weather. Run-to-run variance within "sunny" (0.41 to 0.98) is larger than the gap between strata. One fast, straight, low-parallax stretch fails in any weather.
  • Desert. Heat shimmer is nearly harmless until s5 (0.77): displacement per view is smaller than SIFT's localisation noise. Harsh sun is the worst desert effect (0.34 at s3) because clipped regions have no gradients. Sandstorm behaves like coloured fog.
  • Falling snow is the worst synthetic weather (0.55 at s1): flakes are DoG blobs that compete for the 8,192-feature budget.
  • Synthetic and real conditions are mis-ranked. Synthetic fog and snow at s5 (0.17, 0.04) are harsher than any real daytime condition; synthetic night (0.33 at s5) is far milder than real night (0.02).

9. E2 Escalating noise: analytic vs generative

Why it might fail

Noise creates false keypoints; blur removes real ones. Generative models invent content independently per view, so features can be matched confidently to things that are not there.

Why it might work

Low-strength generative edits keep layout and look faithful. SIFT's coarser octaves average out noise. RANSAC filters inconsistent matches.

Findings

  • Two failure modes. Removal (blur, pixelation, low contrast) lowers registration. Additive damage (noise, JPEG) keeps registration high but ruins accuracy. JPEG s1 registers 99% of images yet scores 0.57, because 8×8 block corners create repeatable but wrong keypoints.
  • Motion blur is the most damaging single corruption (0.60 at 5 px): anisotropic blur shifts keypoints differently in each view.
  • LaMa inpainting is not worse than deletion (0.56 vs 0.48 at s3). SIFT finds few keypoints in smooth fills.
  • Real-ESRGAN regeneration is harmful (0.35 at ×1.5, vs 0.95 for pixelate ×2).
  • SD-Turbo at strength 0.1 gives 0.00 (2 of 50 images register). PSNR is about 20.5 dB and 70% of SIFT keypoints survive, but RANSAC inliers on two Gerrard Hall pairs drop from 344 to 17 and from 157 to 10. Descriptors no longer agree across views because texture is re-drawn independently.

10. E3 Super-resolution before SfM

Why it might fail

Learned SR hallucinates detail independently per image. Invented texture gives keypoints that do not repeat in other views.

Why it might work

More pixels give SIFT more octaves and sharper edges. ESRGAN raised correspondence counts for UAV photogrammetry (Pashaei et al., 2020).

Protocol. Downsample by f ∈ {4, 8} (area interpolation), then upscale back to 1024 px with: nothing (low-res input as is), bicubic, bicubic + unsharp mask (amount 0.8, σ = 0.6f), Real-ESRGAN ×4 (×8 = two passes then resize), or the Stable Diffusion ×4 upscaler (192 px tiles padded to multiples of 8, 20 steps, prompt "a high quality photo"; ×8 = SD ×4 then bicubic ×2).

{{tbl:E3}}
  • Bicubic helps a lot over raw low-res (0.02 → 0.31 at ×4). Unsharp masking adds little (0.32).
  • Generative SR is worse than bicubic: Real-ESRGAN 0.11, SD ×4 0.10. At ×8 every method fails.

11. E4 Tourist crowds

Why it might fail

People are richly textured and win the feature budget. They move between views, so their matches describe wrong geometry. A finite cut-out pool means the same person can appear in several views and give consistent wrong matches.

Why it might work

The background is static and RANSAC rejects matches on moving people. Masks are cheap and supported by COLMAP.

Protocol. People per image at s1–s5: 2, 5, 10, 20, 40. Each person is placed independently per image, foot position below the horizon (45% of height), height 12%–55% of the image growing toward the bottom (perspective), far people first so near ones occlude them, soft alpha edges and half-strength colour matching to the background. Mitigations: mask (dilated person mask as a COLMAP feature mask) and LaMa removal (inpaint the dilated mask before SfM).

{{tbl:E4}}
  • Silent failure: 40 people give 0.04 with 89% registered.
  • Masking (0.32) and LaMa removal (0.32) help equally; neither recovers the facade hidden behind the crowd.

12. E5 Partial contamination

Why it might fail

Incremental SfM builds on early decisions: a badly posed image triangulates bad points that later images register against.

Why it might work

Bad images should simply not register while good ones carry the model; Wang et al. (2024) found COLMAP tolerant at 10% corruption.

Protocol. A seeded random 10, 25, 50, 75 or 100% of images is corrupted with one of: motion blur s4 (30 px), LaMa inpainting s4 (30% area), crowd s5 (40 people), night s4. The rest stay clean.

{{tbl:E5}}
  • Bad images poison good ones. At 10% motion blur, registration falls to 0.83 (more than the 10% blurred) and AUC to 0.60.
  • LaMa-edited images are tolerated up to about 50% (0.84).

13. E6 Features, matcher and mapper

Why it might fail

A learned matcher trained to find correspondences in hard images may also match look-alike structures. Global SfM cannot reject a bad view gradually.

Why it might work

ALIKED and LightGlue are trained with heavy photometric augmentation and lead matching benchmarks (Bonilla et al., 2024).

Protocol. 3 feature/matcher options (SIFT; ALIKED + LightGlue; ALIKED + mutual-NN ratio 0.9) × 2 mappers (incremental, global) × 8 conditions (clean, motion blur s3, Gaussian noise s4, night s3, fog s5, LaMa s3, crowd s4 = 20 people, Real-ESRGAN SR ×8), 3 seeds × 2 scenes each. Mean AUC@10:

{{tbl:E6}}
  • Learned features trade clean accuracy for robustness. SIFT incremental is best on clean (0.96) and on LaMa (0.63). Learned features win under noise (0.46 vs 0.09), crowds (0.34 vs 0.11), night (0.49 vs 0.37) and SR ×8 (0.41 vs 0.00). LightGlue alone survives motion blur (0.28).
  • ALIKED fails on clean images through symmetry. All images register with 0.6–0.7 px reprojection error, but per-image median relative-rotation errors cluster at 89°/91° on South Building and 78°/103° on Gerrard Hall. The same diagnostic gives 0.4° on SIFT runs (scripts/diag_poses.py), so the evaluation is sound. We interpret this as facades registered onto look-alike neighbouring facades; this has not been checked view by view.
  • The matcher is the main culprit. Replacing LightGlue with NN + ratio on the same features raises clean AUC from 0.35 to 0.56 (0.96 on a 14-image South Building subset).
  • GLOMAP is the most symmetry-sensitive. On clean Gerrard Hall it registers all 50 images at 0.35 px reprojection error, but 12 consecutive images (one side of the building) are rotated by about 135°. It wins only in heavy fog with SIFT (0.41 vs 0.20).

14. E7 Cross-season localisation

Why it might fail

Snow cover, foliage and especially night lighting change descriptors.

Why it might work

Buildings and street furniture persist, and the viewpoint is the same, which is SIFT's easiest case.

Protocol. At each of the 4 places, a map from the sunny reference run (40 frames) and 20 query frames from another run are reconstructed jointly (SIFT, exhaustive, incremental, fixed intrinsics). Both sets have ground truth in one ENU frame. We report the fraction of query frames registered into the map model and the AUC@10 over map–query pairs.

{{tbl:E7}}
  • Daytime conditions, including snow cover, localise well (0.76–0.90).
  • Night: 0 of 240 queries registered.

15. Discussion and conjectures

  1. Real night, not weather, is the hard case, and exposure-plus-noise simulation underestimates it. Corruption benchmarks can mis-rank real conditions.
  2. Harm from generative edits scales with invented high-frequency content, not with how much of the image changes or how it looks. LaMa (smooth) is harmless, ESRGAN (fine texture) is harmful, diffusion (all texture) is fatal. Photos passed through AI enhancement may be unusable for 3D.
  3. Super-resolution helps SfM only if it is multi-view consistent. Single-image generative SR hurts.
  4. Many failures are silent (crowds, JPEG, symmetric facades). Registration rate alone is not a health check.
  5. Quality filtering before SfM is worth more than robustness inside it, because early bad registrations poison later ones.
  6. Robustness to degradation and robustness to ambiguity pull in opposite directions. A matcher that finds correspondences in blurred images also finds them between identical-looking windows. Doppelganger-aware verification (Cai et al., 2023) is needed before learned matchers replace SIFT for landmark collections.

16. Limitations

  • Scenes are subsampled (50 and 40 images at 1024 px), which makes them harder than the full sets; comparisons are relative.
  • COLMAP-scene ground truth is a SIFT reconstruction (pseudo ground truth).
  • Boreas strata were labelled by eye; there is one night run and one snowfall run, and sunny/overcast partly reflect how hard each place is.
  • Synthetic corruptions are models; E1 shows they mis-rank real conditions.
  • The ALIKED symmetry interpretation comes from rotation-error patterns and was not checked view by view.
  • SD ×4 upscaling used one seed. Only off-the-shelf models were used; no fine-tuning.

17. Compute and reproducibility

  • No training. SIFT and SfM are optimisation, not learning. ALIKED, LightGlue, LaMa, Real-ESRGAN, SD-Turbo and the SD ×4 upscaler were used with published weights.
  • CPU: a 4-core cloud container ran most SfM and the CPU-feasible generative models (Real-ESRGAN, LaMa). Summed wall time of all logged runs is about 31 hours.
  • GPU: NERSC Perlmutter (NVIDIA A100), Slurm array jobs for SD-Turbo, the SD ×4 upscaler and all ALIKED runs; about 2.8 GPU-hours in total, under the 8-hour budget.
  • Software: COLMAP 4 via pycolmap 4.2.1 (CPU wheel), kornia 0.9.0, PyTorch, diffusers, OpenCV.

19. Related work and references

Gaps this project fills, from our literature review (docs/related_work.md): existing benchmarks evaluate localisation against a fixed map rather than full SfM on single-condition collections; none covers desert conditions; corruption benchmarks exist for classifiers, NeRF and optical flow but not for SfM pose accuracy; SR studies count points rather than measure poses; crowd handling in NeRF and 3DGS assumes SfM already worked; and no study compares feature extractor × SfM back-end under controlled corruption.
